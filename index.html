Presentation structure

Six parts in this order. Aim for 15–20 minutes, followed by 10 minutes of Q&A and discussion.

123456Q&A
  1. 1

    Thesis / hook

    One clear claim, or a question the talk will answer.

    May open with the surprising finding instead.

    1–2 min
  2. 2

    Research summary (context)

    What the audience needs to know about the topic to follow the argument.

    Context only; save the supporting data for the evidence part.

    3 min
  3. 3

    Evidence

    Data, cases and examples that support the thesis. Show the AI-generated artifact here.

    The slides do not count as the artifact.

    4–5 min
  4. 4

    Surprising finding

    One thing that surprised you or changed your view during the research.

    The AI diary is a good source for this.

    2–3 min
  5. 5

    Counter-argument

    The strongest opposing view, held by a reasonable person, with a source, and your response to it.

    No straw men.

    3–4 min
  6. 6

    Conclusion

    Return to the thesis. End with one or two open questions for the room.

    The questions start the discussion.

    1–2 min

Rules for the artifact

  • Make it with AI, for your topic: an infographic, image, web page, video or similar.
  • The presentation slides do not count as the artifact.
  • Show it during the evidence part of your presentation.
  • Make it for the web: a web page, or media a web page can show (images, embedded video or audio, an interactive prototype, a PDF).
  • Hand in a title, a one-sentence summary and a cover image (16:9, at least 1600 px wide) for its tile on the course homepage.
  • Only publish material you have the rights to, and credit every image.

Rules for the diary log and sources

  • Keep the diary while you work: the AI tools you used, your key prompts, what worked, what the AI got wrong, and what you decided yourself.
  • Check AI answers against the pages they point to.
  • List every source you used, with the date you accessed it. AI answers are not sources; the pages they point to are.
  • Hand in the diary with your presentation. It is published next to your artifact.
  • Read it back before you present: it is the best place to find your surprising finding.

At the end of the semester all submissions go on this website: a homepage with one tile per topic, each linking to its artifact and diary log.
